Free Public Transport for Students

Every morning, thousands of students in Indian cities spend a real part of their family's budget just getting to class. I believe city governments should make buses and metro travel free for all school and college students, because it would widen access to education, cut traffic, and cost far less than it seems.

The strongest reason is access. For a family on a modest income, monthly travel passes for two children can cost as much as the electricity bill. When travel is expensive, some students skip coaching classes, libraries or even school on certain days. Free travel removes that hidden fee on education. Delhi made bus travel free for women in 2019, and more women started using buses afterwards, which shows that price really does change who travels.

Free student travel would also reduce congestion. Many parents drive their children to school because buses feel costly or unreliable. If students could travel free, a share of those private trips would move to public transport, which means fewer vehicles at peak hours and cleaner air around schools.

Critics argue that the scheme would drain transport budgets that are already under pressure. This concern is fair, but the cost is smaller than it looks. Students mostly travel at fixed times on routes that already run, so the extra cost is lost fare revenue, not new buses. Cities could also fund the scheme with a small parking surcharge, charging private car users for the road space they take up while paying for an alternative.

Others worry that free passes would be misused. A travel card linked to a student ID and valid only on weekdays would limit misuse without adding much paperwork.

Education should not depend on whether a family can afford the bus. Free student transport is a practical, affordable step that pays back in better attendance, lighter traffic and cleaner air. City governments should introduce it in their next budget.
